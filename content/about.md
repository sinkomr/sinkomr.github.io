I'm Michael Sinko, a physicist in the Bay Area. These days I work on high energy density physics and pulsed power systems at Fisica ATI, and I consult on quantum computing hardware through my company, SQUID Consultants LLC. This site is where I put my public research, projects, and interesting measurements.

The story so far: I grew up in western Pennsylvania, splitting my time between the woods and whatever book I was reading that week. Scouting stuck with me longer than most things do, and I made Eagle Scout before heading off to college.

I studied physics at Miami University, earning a BS and MS. My first year I set up a PCB prototyping line, and I spent the years after characterizing magnetic materials, mostly nanoparticles and thin films. I also discovered intramural broomball, which is played exactly the way you'd guess, and hockey.

My PhD is from Carnegie Mellon, where I worked on 2D materials and superconducting circuits. Most of that happened in cleanrooms and nanofab facilities, so I spent a good chunk of my twenties in a bunny suit. Somewhere in there I adopted two cats, Pico and Nano, and learned to ski.

A postdoc at Sandia National Laboratories brought me west to Livermore, where I set up a nanoimprint lithography system and did UHV work on nickelate superconductors. I stayed in the Bay Area after, first doing quantum device fabrication at Bleximo, then joining Fisica ATI when Bleximo wound down.

When I'm not doing physics I'm usually woodworking, 3D printing, or out in the garden.
