Hi there, this is Michael. I messed with physics from western Pennsylvania all the way to a PhD, spent the better part of a decade in cleanrooms making very small things, and now I work on high energy density physics and pulsed power systems at Fisica ATI, making very large sparks instead. I also do quantum computing hardware consulting through my company, SQUID Consultants LLC (the acronym is very intentional). I plan to dump my research, projects, and interesting measurements here.

Here's the longer version:

I grew up in western Pennsylvania, where I spent most of my time either outside or buried in a book. Scouting was a big part of my childhood, and I stuck with it all the way to Eagle Scout (yes, I can still tie all the knots).

For undergrad I studied physics at Miami University, picking up a BS and MS along the way. My first year there I set up a PCB prototyping line, then spent the rest of my lab time characterizing magnetic materials like nanoparticles and thin films. Outside the lab I played intramural broomball and hockey (broomball is exactly what it sounds like, and it is glorious).

Grad school took me to Carnegie Mellon for a PhD in physics, working on 2D materials and superconducting circuits. In practice that meant a truly enormous number of hours in cleanrooms and nanofab facilities (if you have ever exfoliated 2D materials with Scotch tape, I feel your pain). Along the way I adopted two cats, Pico and Nano, and learned to ski.

After that I headed west to California for a postdoc at Sandia National Laboratories in Livermore, and I've been in the Bay Area ever since. At Sandia I set up a nanoimprint lithography system and did UHV work on nickelate superconductors. From there I went to Bleximo doing quantum device fabrication, and when that adventure wound down I started at Fisica ATI.

Outside physics, I enjoy woodworking, 3D printing, and gardening.
