I'm Michael Sinko, a physicist in the Bay Area. These days I work on high energy density physics and pulsed power systems at Fisica ATI, and I consult on quantum computing hardware through my company, SQUID Consultants LLC. This site is where I put my public research, projects, and interesting measurements.

So far in this experiment we call life: I grew up in western Pennsylvania, splitting my time between the woods and whatever book I was reading that week (not always mutually exclusive; books only add a few pounds to your camping gear, just make sure the library books stay dry). Scouting kept me busy, I didn't burn or cut myself (too much), and I earned my Eagle before heading off to college.

I studied physics at Miami University, earning a BS and MS. My first year I set up a PCB prototyping line, and I spent the years after characterizing magnetic materials, mostly nanoparticles and thin films. This was when I first got to play around with cryogenic liquids and high magnetic fields. Much fun! I also discovered intramural broomball and learned to play ice hockey.

My PhD is from Carnegie Mellon, where I worked on 2D materials and superconducting circuits. Most of that happened in cleanrooms and nanofab facilities, so I spent a good chunk of my twenties in a bunny suit. I was also the grill master for our department's summer cookouts! My two cats, Pico and Nano, kept me company throughout grad school, and I learned to ski in the Appalachians each winter (sans felines).

A postdoc at Sandia National Laboratories brought me west to Livermore, where I set up a nanoimprint lithography system and played with a fancy but frustrating UHV setup. I stayed in the Bay Area afterward, first doing quantum device fabrication at Bleximo, then joining Fisica ATI when Bleximo ceased operations.

When I'm not doing physics, I'm usually woodworking, 3D printing, or out in the garden (which are all still basically physics, right?).
