You listed capacitance and resistance, so inductance completes the set. In both of my fields it is the quantity you fight: parasitic inductance limits how fast a qubit gate or a current pulse can be.

| Scale | Reference point | My encounters |
|-------|-----------------|---------------|
| 10⁻¹² H | Junction and via parasitics; SQUID loop inductances (pH) | Consulting territory |
| 10⁻⁹ H | One millimeter of bond wire (~1 nH/mm) | Why RF engineers fear wirebonds |
| 10⁻⁸ H | An entire multi-megamp machine feed, engineered down to tens of nH | The number pulsed power designers fight for |
| 10⁻⁶ H | RF chokes and boost converters (μH) | |
| 10⁻³ H | Motor windings, audio crossovers (mH) | |
| 10⁰ H | Iron-core inductors and relay coils | |
| 10² H | An MRI superconducting magnet (~100 H) | |
